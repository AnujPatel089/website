SympDirect — ESI Care Routing Assistant

An end-to-end clinical decision-support system for classifying Emergency Severity Index levels 3, 4 and 5 from structured emergency-department intake data. High-acuity ESI 1/2 cases are handled by a separate conservative safety gate, and every model prediction remains reviewable by a clinician.

Business Understanding

The Problem & Why It Matters

Emergency-department triage has to turn symptoms, notes, vitals and demographics into an urgency level in minutes. Under-triage can delay care for patients who are sicker than they appear; over-triage can consume scarce ED capacity. SympDirect is designed as decision support rather than decision replacement.

Safety First

The system targets the difficult ESI 3–5 band and layers transparent safety rules on top of model output for high-risk presentations.

Human in the Loop

Predictions are reviewable. Clinicians can accept or override a recommendation, with the workflow designed around explicit user actions.

Accountability

Assessments, reviews and reports are persisted so actions remain queryable and auditable rather than disappearing inside a black-box prediction.

Data Understanding & Preparation

Clinical Data Without Post-Triage Leakage

Training uses approximately 257,000 ESI 3/4/5 emergency-department visits with 273 engineered features. The model is limited to information available at triage time before disposition is known.

Input Feature Families

  • Demographics such as age and gender
  • Arrival context: mode, month, day and hour
  • Triage vitals: heart rate, blood pressure, respiratory rate, oxygen saturation and temperature
  • Prior ED visit and admission history
  • 190+ one-hot chief-complaint flags
  • Derived measures including shock index, pulse pressure and mean arterial pressure

Leakage & Bias Controls

  • Post-triage columns such as labs, imaging, disposition and admission are blocked programmatically
  • Ethnicity, race, language, religion, marital/employment and insurance status are excluded
  • Numeric medians are fit on the training split only
  • Stratified train/validation/test split is created before preprocessing statistics
  • Raw data and PHI-bearing files are not committed with the application

Methodology

Deployed Model — LightGBM V3

LightGBM was selected for strong tabular performance, efficient multiclass inference, practical handling of missing triage values and mature SHAP support. The deployed V3 adds probability calibration and a wider safety-oriented threshold search.

Key Configuration

Objectivemulticlass, num_class = 3
Learning rate0.02
Leaves63
Class balancingInverse-frequency weights + ESI-5 multiplier sweep
Decision ruleTuned ESI-5 threshold, then arg-max
CalibrationIsotonic regression

Why the Selection Process Matters

  • Up to 800 boosting rounds with early stopping; selected run stopped at 40 rounds
  • Validation safety target: unsafe ESI 3→5 downgrade rate ≤ 0.75%
  • Only threshold/weight candidates meeting the safety constraint are eligible for deployment
  • Calibration is accepted only if it preserves Macro F1, ESI-5 F1 and safety performance
  • Isotonic calibration met that bar for V3; Platt calibration did not
Engineering decision: accuracy alone does not win. Model selection explicitly trades small headline-score gains for safer downgrade behavior, calibrated probabilities and transparent clinical review.

Results & Evaluation

Held-Out Test Performance

Final reported metrics come from a held-out test split that was not used for threshold selection or calibration decisions.

78.52%Accuracy
70.35%Macro F1
54.16%ESI 5 F1
0.57%Unsafe ESI 3→5 rate

What the Errors Show

Most confusion occurs between adjacent ESI 4 and ESI 5 classes—the clinically softer error pattern. Chief complaint and arrival mode are among the strongest feature-importance drivers.

Interpretation

Structured triage data contains useful signal for ESI 3/4 routing. ESI 5 remains the rarest and hardest class, which is why the deployed system uses dedicated weighting, threshold tuning and calibration instead of relying on raw arg-max alone.

Portfolio Context

My Role — Backend / DevOps

My assigned capstone role is Backend / DevOps, so this portfolio case study emphasizes the production-facing engineering layer: the FastAPI serving architecture, database-backed workflow, application integration, model-registry/deployment design, validation behavior and reliability considerations around model serving.

Serving Layer

FastAPI exposes the prediction workflow and coordinates feature building, model inference, safety handling and service logic.

State & Auditability

SQL persistence keeps assessments, reviews and audit events outside the client so browser/mobile state is never the source of truth.

Deployment Reliability

Artifacts, class labels, feature counts and thresholds are validated at startup. Invalid or missing model artifacts fail explicitly rather than silently returning a wrong prediction.

Discussion

Limitations & Next Engineering Steps

Current Limitations

  • ESI 1/2 use a simpler safety-rule gate and are not evaluated as rigorously as the ESI 3/4/5 ML classifier
  • Ambulance-arrival and allergy-complaint subgroups show materially weaker ESI 5 detection
  • No prospective, multi-site or external clinical validation yet
  • SQLite and mock authentication are capstone-grade infrastructure, not suitable for real patient data

Future Work

  • Prospective or multi-site clinical validation before real clinical use
  • Subgroup-aware threshold tuning for identified fairness gaps
  • Production PostgreSQL, real authentication and secrets management
  • Complete and verify iOS packaging; distribute through controlled mobile testing
